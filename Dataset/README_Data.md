# BIBA StyleIQ — Simulated Dataset

> **Disclosure:** This dataset is **simulated**. It's modelled on how an Indian ethnic-wear retailer like BIBA operates, but it is **not BIBA's data**. Stores, suppliers, products, prices and volumes are all invented. It was generated by `Generator/generate_dataset.rb` (fixed seed 2047, so it's fully reproducible).
>
> Suggested wording for GitHub, your resume and interviews: *"BIBA-inspired retail supply chain case study using a simulated dataset."*

Period: **1 Jan 2022 – 31 Dec 2023** (730 days)

**Two formats of the same data:**
- The 7 CSV files: for Power BI Desktop (Windows).
- `BIBA_StyleIQ_Data.xlsx`: all 7 tables in one workbook (one sheet and Excel Table each), for Power BI Online. It also includes pre-built columns: `Dim_Date[season_order]`; `Dim_Product[margin_pct, price_band, price_band_order, lead_time_days]`; `Fact_Purchase_Orders[shipment_type, delay_days, actual_lead_time_days]` with `delivery_status` split into On Time / Late / Open; `Fact_Inventory_Monthly[cover_days, stock_status]`. Built by `Generator/build_excel.rb`.

## Tables (star schema)

| Table | Rows | Grain (one row = …) |
|---|---|---|
| `Dim_Date` | 730 | one calendar day |
| `Dim_Product` | 60 | one SKU (12 per category) |
| `Dim_Store` | 24 | one store (23 physical + Online) |
| `Dim_Supplier` | 8 | one supplier |
| `Fact_Sales` | ~272k | units of one SKU sold in one store on one day |
| `Fact_Inventory_Monthly` | ~30k | one SKU in one store at month end |
| `Fact_Purchase_Orders` | ~23k | one replenishment order |

### Power BI relationships (all one-to-many, single direction)

```
Dim_Date[date]            → Fact_Sales[date]
Dim_Date[date]            → Fact_Inventory_Monthly[month_end_date]
Dim_Date[date]            → Fact_Purchase_Orders[order_date]
Dim_Product[product_id]   → Fact_Sales / Fact_Inventory_Monthly / Fact_Purchase_Orders [product_id]
Dim_Store[store_id]       → Fact_Sales / Fact_Inventory_Monthly / Fact_Purchase_Orders [store_id]
Dim_Supplier[supplier_id] → Fact_Purchase_Orders[supplier_id]
```
Mark `Dim_Date` as the date table. Don't relate `Dim_Supplier` to `Dim_Product`: it would create two filter paths to the purchase orders (ambiguity). Bring the supplier's lead time into `Dim_Product` with `LOOKUPVALUE` instead (see the step-by-step guide, section 5.6).

## Columns

**Dim_Date**: date, year, month_no, month_name, year_month, quarter, fiscal_year (Indian FY, Apr–Mar), week_of_year, weekday_no (Mon=1), weekday, is_weekend, season (Winter Dec–Feb, Summer Mar–May, Monsoon Jun–Sep, Festive Oct–Nov), festival (name during a festival's shopping build-up, else blank), is_sale_period (end-of-season sale, 1–25 Jan and 1–25 Jul), is_wedding_season

**Dim_Product**: product_id, product_name, category (Kurta, Palazzo Set, Salwar Suit, Anarkali, Lehenga), collection (Everyday / Festive / Wedding), fabric, mrp_inr, unit_cost_inr, supplier_id, launch_date (most SKUs 2022-01-01; one drop on 2023-03-01 and one on 2023-09-01 per category)

**Dim_Store**: store_id, store_name, city, state, region (North, West, South, East, Central, Online), store_type (Flagship, Mall, Shop-in-Shop, Online)

**Dim_Supplier**: supplier_id, supplier_name, city, state, specialisation, contracted_lead_time_days

**Fact_Sales**: sale_id, date, store_id, product_id, units_sold, mrp_inr, discount_pct, gross_revenue_inr (units × MRP), net_revenue_inr (after discount), cogs_inr (units × unit cost)

**Fact_Inventory_Monthly**: month_end_date, store_id, product_id, opening_stock, units_received, units_sold, closing_stock, planned_demand_units (what the planner expected), actual_demand_units (what customers wanted), est_lost_sales_units (demand that couldn't be met because stock was zero), stockout_days, closing_stock_value_cost_inr

Check: opening_stock + units_received − units_sold = closing_stock on every row.

**Fact_Purchase_Orders**: po_id, order_date, store_id, product_id, supplier_id, qty_ordered, expected_delivery_date (order date + contracted lead time), actual_delivery_date, qty_received, unit_cost_inr, po_value_inr, delivery_status (On Time / Late / … - Short Shipped / Open). Orders still in transit on 31 Dec 2023 are "Open" with blank delivery fields.

## How the data behaves (the patterns you can discover in Power BI)

The simulation builds in patterns a real ethnic-wear retailer would face, so your dashboards have real things to find:

- **Festive peak:** Oct–Nov is the biggest period, driven by the build-up to Diwali, with regional peaks for Durga Puja (East), Navratri (West/Central), Onam and Pongal (South), and Karva Chauth (North/Central). Smaller bumps for Eid and Raksha Bandhan.
- **Wedding seasons** (mid-Nov–mid-Dec, mid-Jan–Feb, mid-Apr–May) lift Lehenga, Anarkali and Wedding-collection sales.
- **Summer** (Apr–Jun) lifts cotton Kurtas and Palazzo Sets; **monsoon** (Jun–Aug) is the slowest period.
- **End-of-season sales** in January and July: big discounts and volume spikes, mostly on Everyday products.
- **Regional tastes:** North is strongest for Lehenga and Salwar Suits, South for Kurtas, West for Anarkalis and Palazzo Sets.
- **Weekends** are busier in physical stores. **Online** grows ~70% from the first half of 2022 to the second half of 2023, and 2023 is ~10% up on 2022.
- **The planning flaw (the core business problem):** stores reorder against a *flat* average demand plan with a reorder point, and only a modest pre-festive stock build. That plan can't keep up with festive and wedding peaks, so stockouts spike in May and November. Meanwhile some SKUs were over-planned (planned demand ≈ 1.3–1.6× actual) and pile up as excess stock, and one bestseller per category is under-planned.
- **Supplier reliability varies** widely: high-volume cotton suppliers are mostly on time, while bridal and handcraft suppliers have long lead times and are often late or short-ship.

## Things the data does *not* include

No customer data, no returns, no store-to-store transfers, no markdowns beyond the discount rules above. `est_lost_sales_units` is something a real retailer can only estimate; here we know it exactly because it's a simulation.
